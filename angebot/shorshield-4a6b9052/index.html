01

Product and market

The product

ShorShield is passwordless authentication for regulated companies in Germany, Austria and Switzerland. Employees log in with a passkey on their own device instead of a password or a one-time code. Credentials stay on that device and never reach ShorShield's servers. The cryptographic core is post-quantum, and the service runs entirely in the EU with no dependency on US cloud providers.

The buyers are CISOs, IT risk managers and compliance leads at banks, Sparkassen, Volksbanken, insurers, and payment and e-money institutions. They buy when something forces them to: a supervisory review, an internal audit finding or the yearly ICT risk assessment.

What is happening in the market

  • DORA has applied since 17 January 2025. The ICT risk RTS (2024/1774, Art. 21) requires strong authentication “in accordance with leading practices” for remote access, privileged access, critical functions and publicly accessible systems. Supervisors and internal audit now check this.
  • Every ICT provider goes into the register of information. The authentication service is one of them, so jurisdiction, data location and an exit plan come up in every vendor assessment.
  • One-time codes are no longer safe. Phishing kits relay SMS and push codes in real time, so classic MFA gets harder to defend as “leading practice”.
  • Passkeys have gone mainstream. Apple, Google and Microsoft support them, and employees already know the login flow from their private accounts.
  • Post-quantum is on the radar, without budget yet. About 320 searches a month in Germany for “Post-Quanten-Kryptographie”. It strengthens the long-term argument but rarely triggers a purchase.

What buyers say

  • We already have MFA. The requirement looks done to them.
  • Great idea, but no budget this year. Security upgrades compete with mandatory projects.
  • A new vendor is a new third-party risk. Small providers have to prove they will be around.
  • Show me what the auditor will accept. They want evidence first and features second.

Buyer pain points

  • An open audit finding on authentication
  • Evidence collected by hand before every audit
  • Shared admin accounts that nobody can attribute
  • A phishing incident that had to be reported
  • An entry in the register of information for every provider

Strengths

  • Matches the DORA wording: four access types (Art. 21), logging (Art. 12), one identity per person (Art. 20)
  • EU only, no US jurisdiction: easier third-party assessment
  • No credential data on servers: less to protect, less to explain
  • Post-quantum core: a future-proof argument for long-lived infrastructure
  • Founder with attack experience: real attack chains as credibility in content

Weaknesses

  • No customer references or case studies yet
  • Young vendor: banks ask about continuity and exit plans
  • Sales only through the personal network so far, no inbound
  • Long buying cycles and procurement at banks
  • Crowded category: Microsoft Entra ID, Okta and Ping offer passwordless login, Yubico sells hardware keys, and Nevis serves banks in Switzerland
What this means for marketing

Lead with the audit and back every claim with the legal text. Open the conversation with something free and useful (a DORA checklist), make the first paid step small and fixed in price (a gap assessment), and turn the first design partners into the references that are missing today.
