Why now: what DORA actually asks for

DORA has applied since 17 January 2025. The ICT risk RTS requires strong authentication “in accordance with leading practices”, which leaves each institution to prove what leading practice means today. That wording is the hook for all content.

DORA

Most DORA conversations about authentication stop at “we have MFA”. The RTS on ICT risk management asks for more than that. Article 21 requires strong authentication based on leading practices for four kinds of access: remote access to the network, privileged access, systems that support critical or important functions, and anything publicly accessible.

The word that will matter in your next audit is “leading”. An SMS code was leading practice ten years ago. Today a real-time phishing kit can relay it in seconds.

I put together a one-page map of the four access types with the questions an auditor tends to ask for each. Happy to send it to anyone working on DORA in a bank or payment institution.

Attack

Last year I built an end-to-end AWS attack chain for a security vendor demo. The step that worked most reliably was the boring one at the start: a credential that could be reused.

Everything after it, privilege escalation through a Lambda role, persistence through a new IAM user, ransomware via an attacker-held KMS key, depended on that first login working. Phishing-resistant authentication removes that step for the human side of the chain.
